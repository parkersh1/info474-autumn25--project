By Karl Lee, Raizel Lagunero, and Parker Huang
Every day, thousands of drivers take to the road without thinking about how weather, road design, or small decisions behind the wheel can change the outcome of a drive. In this interactive article, we explore patterns and relationships in real traffic incident data from Washington State to uncover how environmental and behavioral factors, such as rain and intersection type, can influence both the likelihood and severity of accidents. By visualizing these trends, we aim to give Washington State drivers a clearer understanding of the risk factors involved and help promote safer, more mindful driving habits.
Accident Severity Distribution in Washington State
Understanding how severe traffic incidents tend to be is an important first step in recognizing the risks drivers face on Washington’s roads. This bar chart shows how often crashes fall into each severity category, from minor (Level 1) to the most serious (Level 4). The distribution highlights how much more frequently certain severity levels occur, offering a clear snapshot of statewide roadway conditions and the relative seriousness of reported incidents.
Severity and Incident Distribution Across Major Washington Cities
This heatmap shows where traffic incidents occur across Washington and how severe they tend to be. Each cell represents a small geographic area, with its color indicating the typical severity of crashes and its darkness showing how frequently collisions happen there. Together, these patterns reveal where crashes are most concentrated and where higher-severity incidents are more common, offering a clear statewide view of roadway risk.
Road Visibility
Road visibility plays a crucial role in driving safety, as it directly affects a driver's ability to see and respond to road conditions, obstacles, and other vehicles. While poor visibility is a known hazard, this chart reminds us that most driving happens when visibility is good—so staying alert, avoiding distractions, and practicing safe habits is essential at all times. Visibility alone doesn’t guarantee safety, and being mindful of your surroundings in every condition can significantly reduce your risk on the road.
Weather Conditions
Weather conditions significantly impact driving safety, influencing both the likelihood and severity of traffic incidents. This chart illustrates how different weather scenarios correlate with accident severity in Washington State. Notably, while clear weather accounts for the majority of accidents due to its prevalence, adverse conditions like rain and fog are associated with higher average severity levels. This highlights the increased risks drivers face during poor weather, emphasizing the importance of cautious driving and preparedness in such conditions.
Accident Frequency Among Traffic Intersection Types
This chart compares how often crashes in Washington State occur near different types of intersections and traffic features, such as stop signs, roundabouts, and traffic signals. Calming designs like speed bumps and roundabouts appear much less often in the crash data, while more conventional intersections account for a larger share of collisions. Reading across the chart highlights which features are most commonly present when crashes happen and where drivers may want to use extra caution.
Traffic Features
Crossings, traffic signals, stations, and junctions appear at the top because they involve frequent stopping, turning, or interactions with pedestrians—situations where driver attention and timing matter most. For drivers, this highlights how important it is to stay alert in these areas, reduce speed, and be prepared for sudden changes in traffic flow. Even familiar intersections can be high-risk if drivers aren’t paying full attention.
Conclusion
Taken together, these visual patterns reveal how closely traffic incidents in Washington State are tied to the environments in which they occur and the choices drivers make every day. Weather, traffic intersection design, visibility, and intersection type all shape the likelihood and severity of crashes, more often than not in predictable ways. By recognizing these relationships, drivers can make more informed decisions, whether that means slowing down in the rain, staying alert at high-risk intersections, or anticipating reduced visibility at certain times of day. Ultimately, understanding these data-driven insights empowers all of us to navigate Washington’s roads with greater awareness and contribute to a safer transportation landscape for everyone.
