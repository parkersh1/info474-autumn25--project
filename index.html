By Karl Lee, Raizel Lagunero, and Parker Huang
Every day, thousands of Washington drivers take to the road without thinking about how weather, road design, or small decisions behind the wheel can change the outcome of a drive. In this interactive article, we explore patterns and relationships in real traffic incident data from Washington State to uncover how environmental and behavioral factors, such as rain and intersection type, can influence both the likelihood and severity of accidents. By visualizing these trends, we aim to give Washington State drivers a clearer understanding of the risk factors involved and help promote safer, more mindful driving habits.
About Severity Levels
Severity levels classify how serious a traffic incident is, ranging from minor disruptions to life-threatening crashes. Level 1 represents the least severe events, such as minor damage or low-impact collisions. Level 2 includes moderate incidents that may involve noticeable damage or minor injuries. Level 3 reflects more serious crashes with significant damage or injuries. Level 4 represents the most severe incidents, often involving major injuries or critical outcomes.

Severity and Incident Distribution Across Washington State (2023)
This heatmap provides a geographic view of where crashes occur across Washington and how severe they tend to be. Each grid cell corresponds to a specific area of the state, with color showing the typical severity of crashes and darkness indicating how often collisions happen there. The added state outline and latitude–longitude cues help ground the visualization in real geography, while hover-over details reveal crash counts and average severity for any location. Together, these elements highlight where crashes cluster, where severity is highest, and how risk varies across Washington’s road network.
Fatal Accidents
Fatal accidents are the most severe type of traffic incident, resulting in loss of life. This bar chart shows the distribution of fatal accidents in Washington State per month. The data reveals patterns in when fatal accidents are more likely to occur, highlighting potential seasonal or monthly trends. January shows high fatal accidents, possibly due to winter weather conditions, while other months show lower numbers, potentially due to better driving conditions.
Weather Conditions (2023)
Weather conditions significantly impact driving safety, influencing both the likelihood and severity of traffic incidents. This chart illustrates how different weather scenarios correlate with accident severity in Washington State. Notably, while clear weather accounts for the majority of accidents due to its prevalence, adverse conditions like rain and fog are associated with higher average severity levels. This highlights the increased risks drivers face during poor weather, emphasizing the importance of cautious driving and preparedness in such conditions.

Traffic Features (2023)
Crossings, traffic signals, stations, and junctions appear at the top because they involve frequent stopping, turning, or interactions with pedestrians—situations where driver attention and timing matter most. For drivers, this highlights how important it is to stay alert in these areas, reduce speed, and be prepared for sudden changes in traffic flow. Even familiar intersections can be high-risk if drivers aren’t paying full attention.
Conclusion
Taken together, these visual patterns reveal how closely traffic incidents in Washington State are tied to the environments in which they occur and the choices drivers make every day. Weather, traffic intersection design, visibility, and intersection type all shape the likelihood and severity of crashes, more often than not in predictable ways. By recognizing these relationships, drivers can make more informed decisions, whether that means slowing down in the rain, staying alert at high-risk intersections, or anticipating reduced visibility at certain times of day. Ultimately, understanding these data-driven insights empowers all of us to navigate Washington’s roads with greater awareness and contribute to a safer transportation landscape for everyone.
