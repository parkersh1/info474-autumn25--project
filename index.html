By Karl Lee, Raizel Lagunero,and Parker Huang
Every day, thousands of drivers take to the road without thinking about how weather, road design, or small decisions behind the wheel can change the outcome of a drive. In this interactive article, we explore patterns and relationships in real traffic incident data from Washington State to uncover how environmental and behavioral factors, such as rain and intersection type, can influence both the likelihood and severity of accidents. By visualizing these trends, we aim to give drivers a clearer understanding of the risk factors involved and help promote safer, more mindful driving habits.
Road Visibility
Road visibility varies throughout the day and under different weather conditions.
Environment
Environmental conditions such as heavy rain, fog, or low visibility often correlate with higher car incident severity because they reduce driver reaction time and increase the likelihood of multi-vehicle collisions. Conversely, clear weather and good visibility generally correspond to less severe accidents, highlighting how environmental factors amplify risk on the road.
Accident Proportions Among Different Traffic Types
These charts show the proportion of driving accidents in Washington State (2023) in the "most dangerous" vs "safest" intersection or traffic feature categories, based on data from the Washington State Department of Transportation. The top chart shows the number of accidents that occurred at regular traffic crossings. The bottom chart shows the number of accidents that occurred at roundabouts. The data indicates that accidents are significantly more frequent at traffic crossings versus roundabouts, suggesting that intersection design plays a significant role in roadway safety. However, it is important to note that there is also an abundantly smaller number of roundabouts in Washington State compared to regular trafic crossings.
Accident Frequency Among Traffic Intersection Types
This chart shows how often driving accidents in Washington State involved specific intersection or traffic features (e.g., stop signs, roundabouts, traffic signals). Perhaps unsurprisingly, there were significantly less accidents when safer traffic features were involved, such as speed bumps, roundabouts, and specific traffic calming signs. Based off this chart, one can compare which intersection types and traffic controls are commonly present in collisions and to inform safer driving choices—for example, by paying extra attention where the data shows higher frequencies.
Severity and Incident Distribution Across Major Washington Cities
This visualization provides an overview of how traffic incidents are distributed across major cities in Washington State. Each circle represents a city, with its size showing the total number of recorded collisions and its color reflecting the average severity level. Even with these simplified positions, clear patterns begin to appear in how accident volume and severity differ from place to place. In future versions of this project, we plan to replace this schematic layout with a fully accurate, interactive map so users can explore statewide traffic patterns in greater detail and gain a clearer understanding of how location influences roadway safety.
Accident Severity Distribution in Washington State
Understanding how severe traffic incidents tend to be is an important first step in recognizing the risks drivers face on Washington’s roads. This bar chart shows the distribution of accident severity levels, ranging from minor (Level 1) to the most serious (Level 4). Even in this simple overview, patterns begin to emerge about how frequently more serious crashes occur compared to minor ones. In future versions of this project, we plan to introduce filtering options such as sorting by county so users can explore how severity levels vary across different regions of Washington State and gain a clearer and more localized understanding of roadway safety.
